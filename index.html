Case 03Research Sample
Content Strategy · Educational Content

Why short-form content works — and where it doesn't

A carousel-style breakdown of why short-form has become central to brand content strategy.

Read the analysis
Key Question

Why has short-form content become such an important format for brands trying to reach an audience?

Research Snapshot

1. Most people scroll on mobile, in short, interrupted moments — content built for a five-minute read often loses them before the point lands.
2. The first few seconds carry the most weight, since that's the window a viewer decides whether to keep watching or scroll away.
3. Short formats are easier to reshare, which lets useful or entertaining content travel further through a network on its own.
4. Platform algorithms tend to favor formats that keep people watching, which has pushed brands to compress ideas rather than expand them.

(Illustrative observations based on widely discussed digital marketing patterns, not proprietary data.)

Key Takeaway

Short-form isn't just a trend — it fits how attention actually behaves on mobile. Marketers benefit from treating it as a discipline in compression: saying the essential thing first, then earning the rest of the viewer's time.

Research Approach Identify the question → gather credible perspectives → compare findings → extract insights → translate into content
Case 04Independent Portfolio Analysis
Brand Breakdown

How Duolingo turns personality into marketing

An independent analysis of Duolingo's social content strategy, based on public brand behavior — not internal company information.

Read the breakdown
Brand Personality

Duolingo leans into humor and an exaggerated, slightly chaotic mascot personality that's instantly recognizable, even out of context.

Audience

Primarily a younger, terminally-online audience that engages with brands the way they engage with other creators — through memes and in-jokes, not announcements.

Content Strategy

Social content is built to feel native to each platform rather than repurposed from a press release, which keeps it from reading as an ad.

Voice

Playful, self-aware, and occasionally unhinged — a tone few education brands would risk, which is exactly why it stands out.

Community Strategy

The brand treats comments and reactions as part of the content itself, often replying in the same voice, which turns the audience into participants rather than viewers.

Key Lessons

1. A consistent personality is more memorable than a polished message. 2. Native, platform-first content outperforms repurposed content. 3. Replying in-voice turns an audience into a community.

Framework Observation → Why it works → Marketing lesson
